Theory of evolution

Every creature here starts as a single number: a seed. From that seed the lab derives a spine, a curled tail, a crown of dorsal spikes, and a skin pattern — then lets color and motion drift over time. Change one trait and the whole organism re-computes around it. That is the only rule: form follows formula, and formula follows chance.

Nothing on this page is an image file. The chameleon you see is geometry — spirals, offset curves, and deterministic randomness — recomputed on every frame, in your browser, from scratch.

About

Kumaleon Lab is a small generative-art experiment built inside the LMDJ repository as a product-neutral web lab. It is an original, code-only homage to generative creature sites: a single canvas, a seeded random engine, and a renderer that turns mathematics into a living-looking animal.

There is no backend, no account, and no tracking. The static server binds to loopback, sends cross-origin isolation headers, and stores nothing.

Artist

The artist here is an algorithm. Humans chose the palette names, the curve families, and the constraints — but each individual creature is hatched by a pseudorandom generator that no one directed by hand. The lab's job is to make that collaboration visible: press 🎲 and watch authorship transfer to chance.

Playground

The four icons above the fold are the whole instrument. Palette shifts the color genome, pattern rewrites the skin, the die rolls a brand-new seed, and the timer changes the creature's gait. The readout names the current genome so you can find your way back to a favorite.

History

  2. 0.2 — The creature learned to breathe, blink, and watch the room.
  3. v0.1 — First hatch: one spiral tail, one canvas, one seed.

Hatch

Ready for a different animal? Rolling the die re-derives every trait from a fresh seed — same species, new individual.

Tip: the readout shows the current seed, so you can keep the ones you love.
